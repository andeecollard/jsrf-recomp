2. WHAT I GOT WRONG, AND WHAT CAUGHT IT
====================================================================

Three of them, all caught by measurement rather than by review, and all three
would have sent a later session somewhere expensive.

THE COLLAPSED-Q READING, AND IT IS THE IMPORTANT ONE. Mode 4 showed TEXCOORD0
flat (fract(q)=0.5 everywhere) and "out of range" (blue everywhere), and the
shader's own comment names that pattern as the collapsed-q defect: one corner
texel smeared over the primitive. I reported it as such. IT IS NOT.

  Mode 4's `oor` flag tests q against [0,1]. The Load screen's texture is
  LINEAR, and linear textures take UNNORMALISED coordinates, 0..640 and
  0..480. So blue everywhere is the CORRECT result and indicates nothing at
  all. And fract(q)=0.5 uniformly is exactly what a 1:1 full-screen blit with
  half-texel centring produces -- at pixel x the coordinate is x+0.5, so
  fract is 0.5 at every pixel.

  THE COORDINATES WERE RIGHT THE WHOLE TIME, and our own code already knew
  it: nv2a_metal.m:674 clamps uv to [0,w]x[0,h] when linear, and
  nv2a_texture_copy.c:725 says "Nonlinear textures use normalized
  coordinates, unlike image rectangles."

  WHAT THIS COSTS A LATER SESSION IF UNREAD: mode 4 is now a TRAP on any
  linear texture. Do not read its blue as a defect without checking the
  stage's format first.

THE TRUNCATING-PARSER READING. The differential's 25 missing methods included
texture stage 3 (0x1BC0 x8160) and transform constants 16-23
(0x0BC0-0x0BDC), and our own lists stop exactly one slot short in BOTH cases
-- the precise shape of a parser that truncates. It is not one. Bucketed by
flip position, every single one stops dead at flip 300 and never appears
again in the remaining 2,388 flips. They belong to the boot and intro phase,
which xemu spends far longer in: it loads off a DVD image and played the NOW
LOADING animation, ours loads off an SSD. A SCENE DIFFERENCE, which is what
CLAUDE.md's scene-match rule exists to catch.

  Checked before concluding, and both innocent explanations died too: the
  pair table holds PB_MAX_METHODS=4096 so the 513 is genuine saturation and
  not a recording cap, and nv2a_pb_exec.c:5333 already decodes the whole
  0x0B80-0x0BFC constant window.

THE ARM-DIFFERENCE HYPOTHESIS. Night 2 recorded that on the CPU-interpreter
arm ZERO of 7,454 textured draws carry one coordinate on every vertex. I read
that as localising a coordinate collapse to the GPU vertex path and predicted
mode 4 would look different under RECOMP_METAL_VSH=0. It does not. The CPU
arm genuinely engaged ([METAL] vsh: CPU interpreter, 472 CPU draws, 0 GPU),
armed on the Load screen at 28.0 draws/flip, and produced a picture
BYTE-IDENTICAL in colour distribution to the GPU arm -- RGB(128,128,248) and
RGB(120,124,248), 1092 and 1091 samples, the same two values in the same
proportions. Refuted, and the player called the approach before I did.

  With section 2's first correction this is no longer even a puzzle: the
  coordinates are correct on both arms because they were always correct.

AND ONE FROM THE INSTRUMENT SIDE: the first RECOMP_FRAG_FORCE_ON_BLACK armed
at t=18 on a boot transition and painted the title white before it could be
navigated. The assumption under it -- "a menu is never all-zero" -- was mine
and said nothing about the BOOT. See section 3.

3. THE INSTRUMENTS BUILT, ALL COMMITTED

src/kernel/d3d8_ring.{h,c}
  d3d8_ring_read_target() -- the guest's own render target, depth stencil and
  back buffer 0, with D3DSurface field offsets carrying the disassembly that
  derived them.

  IT VALIDATES ITSELF, AND IT HAS TO. A null or stale device pointer would
  read as a MISMATCH -- as the answer, and the wrong one. So every read also
  takes the write cursor at +0x00 and the ring bounds at +0x24/+0x28 and
  checks lo < hi and lo <= put <= hi. When that fails the line says
  "FIX THE PROBE, NOT THE RENDERER" and gives NO verdict.

src/kernel/nv2a_pb_exec.c

4. THE ONE RUN THAT GATES THE FIX, AND WHY I DID NOT JUST FIX IT
====================================================================

I WENT LOOKING FOR THE MISSING READ-BACK AND IT ALREADY EXISTS:

  nv2a_metal_sync()        writes the BOUND surface back to guest RAM
  nv2a_metal_sync_range()  additionally pays any cache slot that OWES guest
                           RAM and overlaps a named range
  and the backend already reads back "at a flip, a clear, a capture, a
  surface [target] change" (nv2a_metal.m:76), with the rebind path syncing
  when surface_target != target.

Render-to-texture REQUIRES a target change -- you cannot render into
0x03ec4000 and sample it in the same bind -- so the write-back a naive fix
would add is plausibly already happening. Adding a second one would be
patching over a bug that lives elsewhere, with no way to tell a real fix from
a coincidence. That is CLAUDE.md's own rule: do not patch around a gate until
a run proves which value is wrong.

ALSO RULED OUT BEFORE PROPOSING ANYTHING: RECOMP_METAL_DEFER_SWAP ships OFF
("THIS CHANGES WHEN GUEST RAM BECOMES CORRECT, which is why it ships off")
and our Load-menu runs contain zero DEFER lines. So surface_pay_debt_for_range
had nothing to pay and the deferred-swap path is NOT the culprit.

THE RUN: our side at the Load menu with

    RECOMP_SURFACE_CENSUS   what the GPU holds beside what guest RAM holds,
                            "and where they disagree is where the frame goes
                            missing"
    RECOMP_PB_SCAN=1        because "our Load screen binds one of OUR OWN
                            surfaces as a texture" is still INFERRED from the
                            guest being identical, not measured

IT SPLITS THREE WAYS AND ONLY ONE OF THEM IS THE DOZEN-LINE FIX:

  GPU has the picture, guest RAM black -> the read-back is not reaching this
        surface. Small and localised: extend the overlap check on the texture
        upload path (nv2a_metal.m:4879 is where it would go).
  both black -> we never rendered it correctly in the first place. A
        different bug, upstream of coherency.
  guest RAM has it -> the RTT theory is wrong and the fault is in the
        texture BINDING, not its contents.

6. THE XEMU RIG, WHICH NOW WORKS
====================================================================

xemu 0.8.136 at /Applications/xemu.app. Prerequisites all present and
verified: mcpx_1.0.bin, Complex_4627.bin, xbox_hdd.qcow2, the JSRF ISO,
eeprom.bin.

THE BUG THAT STOPPED THE FIRST CAPTURE DEAD, and it is in our script, not in
xemu. xemu 0.8.136 CONSTRUCTS ITS OWN QEMU LAUNCH PARAMETERS from xemu.toml
and then APPENDS whatever argv it is given, so every -machine, -bios, -device
and -drive the script passed arrived twice:

    xemu: -drive index=0,media=disk,file=...qcow2,locked=on:
          drive with bus=0, unit=0 (index=0) exists

Its own line is in xemu-stdout.log under "Created QEMU launch parameters" and
already carries the right bootrom, BIOS, EEPROM, HDD and ISO. The script now
passes -trace ALONE; --full-argv restores the old behaviour. The path checks
stay, because a differential against the wrong ISO is worse than none.

SIZING, MEASURED: the `full` preset writes ~8-10 MB/s and 10.5M lines in 77 s.
--max-mb 768 stops it at ~77 s, which is NOT enough for a person to navigate
anywhere. For a driven capture use --seconds 300 --max-mb 6000; the time
bound binds and the trace lands around 1.8 GB. Tracing makes the emulation
visibly slow -- that is the trace, not the scene.

ONE TRANSIENT CRASH: the first Load-menu attempt died in 1 s with a real
crash report (~/Library/Logs/DiagnosticReports/xemu-2026-09-21-165902.ips).
It ran fine on the retry. Worth knowing before diagnosing a script that is
not broken.

TRACE FORMAT, because the field indices cost two attempts:
    nv2a_pgraph_method <chan>: <class> -> <method> <name> <param>
    $3 = class (0x97 is the 3D class), $5 = method, $6 = NAME, $7 = PARAM.
macOS awk has NO strtonum -- match the hex strings.

WHAT THE ORACLE CAN AND CANNOT SAY, unchanged from the plan: it is ground
truth for WHAT THE GUEST SUBMITTED and worthless for anything about our host
code. Methods on xemu that never reach us mean our parse is losing them; the
same stream on both sides means the fault is in our backend.

what distinguishes them -- recorded so nobody re-derives it as a lead.
